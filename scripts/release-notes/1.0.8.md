# Daisy 1.0.8

- **Daisy for iPhone can now sync with this Mac — off by default.** Nothing leaves your Mac until you turn it on in Settings → General → Sync, and turning it on first shows you exactly what goes where: the text of your sessions (transcript, summary, title, date, folder, speaker names, screenshots) into the private CloudKit database of your own Apple ID, end-to-end encrypted when Advanced Data Protection is on. Recordings never go through the cloud: the iPhone hands them to this Mac directly on the local network, only when asked, and only if you have paired the two. Turning sync off stops sending; "Delete my data from iCloud" removes what was sent.
- **Sessions recorded on the iPhone get their speakers on the Mac.** When the phone hands a recording over, the Mac separates the voices, finds you by your voice profile and labels everyone else — and the names ride back to the phone as text. A phone session is marked `daisy_origin: iphone` in its transcript.
- **Folders are shared with the iPhone.** Create, rename or delete a folder on either device and the other follows; a session moved into a folder on the phone lands in the same folder here.
- **Deleting a session on one device moves the other device's copy to a hidden `.daisy-trash` folder beside your sessions** — kept for 30 days, never silently destroyed. A folder that is merely missing (moved by hand, on a disconnected disk) is never treated as a deletion.
- **Export as `.daisy`.** Right-click a session (or several) in the Library → Export… produces a `.daisy` archive the iPhone opens directly. The older `.daisysession` name still opens.
- **Photos added after the recording** appear in the session with a "added later" mark, and text edits made on the phone survive a re-transcription here.
- **One button vocabulary across the app** — ink and gray, amber only for a live microphone — and the brand reads "addicted", lowercase, in About.
- Your provider keys, Notion and Google connections move to the shared, synchronizable keychain so the iPhone can use the same keys; the old entries are kept, so going back to an earlier version keeps everything connected.
